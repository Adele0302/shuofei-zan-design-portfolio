# Sky-Green Glaze Across Four Kilns of the Northern Song Ru-Kiln System

**四窑系天青釉色比较研究**

*Ceramic Materials · Archaeometry · Heritage Science · Color Formation*

## Project snapshot

This co-authored study examines why sky-green celadon glazes from four kilns in the Northern Song Ru-kiln system show differences in hue, brightness, and visual texture. It brings together colorimetric, compositional, phase, oxidation-state, and microstructural evidence to interpret those differences.

## Research question

How do glaze chemistry, iron oxidation states, crystalline phases, and microstructure jointly relate to the visible color and brightness of sky-green porcelain from different kilns within the same historical production system?

## Study scope

The comparison covers samples from four Northern Song Ru-system kiln sites:

- Qingliangsi
- Donggou
- Yanhedian
- Duandian

Rather than treating glaze color as a purely visual attribute, the study considers it alongside chemical composition and structural evidence.

## Analytical approach

The article reports a multi-method characterization workflow:

- **Colorimetry** — measurement of glaze color using L*, a*, and b* values.
- **X-ray fluorescence (XRF)** — analysis of glaze chemical composition.
- **X-ray diffraction (XRD)** — identification of crystalline phases.
- **X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)** — investigation of elemental valence states, including iron.
- **Scanning electron microscopy with energy-dispersive spectroscopy (SEM/EDS)** — examination of microstructure and associated elemental information.

These are methods used in the collaborative study; this list does not imply that every co-author personally operated every instrument.

## Key findings reported in the study

### 1. Composition and brightness

Across the Yanhedian, Donggou, Duandian, and Qingliangsi samples, the study reports a successive decrease in Fe₂O₃ content accompanied by a successive increase in brightness.

### 2. Microstructure and the milky appearance

The higher brightness and milky visual texture of the Qingliangsi glaze were associated with small, dense bubbles, a greater presence of mullite crystals, and light scattering related to the phase-separated structure.

### 3. Hue, iron valence, and firing conditions

Differences in the Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ ratio and P₂O₅ content were associated with a change in glaze appearance from greenish-blue toward greenish-yellow across the sample sequence. The study also reports that higher CaO content in the Qingliangsi and Duandian glazes deepened the blue hue.

## Why this research matters

- **Evidence integration:** interprets visual appearance through converging chemical, phase, oxidation-state, and microstructural evidence.
- **Comparative ceramic archaeology:** examines shared characteristics and differences across multiple kiln sites rather than treating one kiln in isolation.
- **Heritage and technology:** contributes evidence relevant to understanding historical firing technology, archaeological interpretation, and research on Ru-system celadon replication and development.
